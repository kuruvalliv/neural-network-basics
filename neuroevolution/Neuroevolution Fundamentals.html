The Fundamentals of Neuroevolution

Let a population, not a gradient, shape a neural network: first its weights, then its very structure, growing from minimal to just-complex-enough.

Neuroevolution needs only a fitness function. It copes with non-differentiable parts and keeps a diverse population.

§3.1

Two questions sort every method

What evolves? Only the weights of a fixed network, or weights and structure (a TWEANN)?

How is it written down? The genotype can spell out every connection (direct) or hold a compact recipe that grows the network (indirect).

Direct vs indirect: genome size

Left: a direct genome, one bit per possible connection. Right: an indirect genome with one gene, k, and a rule: “connect each neuron to neighbours within k”. Both give the same matrix.

direct genome: genes
indirect genome: gene + rule

Try mutating: a direct mutation flips one connection. An indirect mutation changes k, and with it the whole pattern.

Trade-off. Direct: precise and simple, but length grows like n², and repeated patterns must be found one gene at a time. Indirect: compact and captures regular patterns, but one mutation has broad, nonlinear effects.
Skip-ahead. Indirect encodings get a whole chapter (Ch. 4, e.g. HyperNEAT). Hold onto the picture above. The rest of this chapter is direct encoding.
For your apps. An evolved code file is a direct encoding. Evolving a prompt or template that generates code is closer to indirect: small edits, big effects.
Why does direct-encoding length grow quadratically?

With dense connectivity, each of n neurons may connect to each other one, so about n² connection genes.

When should you prefer an indirect encoding?

When the solution should have symmetry or repeated motifs, as in many sensorimotor tasks. For irregular solutions, direct often wins.

§3.2

Case study: a walker that has to generalize

The book evolves a 24-input, 4-output network for BipedalWalkerHardcore with CMA-ES. This runs a tiny version of the same experiment in your browser: random terrain, a small fixed network (3→5→2), and a GA.

Fitness is the sum of rewards over one rollout. Only the final total matters, not per-step credit, so sparse or delayed reward is fine.

What to watch. At K=1 the training curve sits near 300 but the test curve stays low. The champion was lucky, drawing a flat terrain. At K=16 both curves meet. The cost is 16× more episodes. Same lesson as the book: about 220–230 with single trials, over 300 with 16-trial averages.

Skip-ahead. CMA-ES and other ES variants are covered in Ch. 2 (§2.2). The idea here doesn't depend on which EA you use.
For your apps. If you score an embedding-based decision policy on a few hand-picked queries, it can get lucky. Average over K sampled scenarios. K is how you trade compute for robustness.
Why does K=1 overestimate quality?

Selection picks the maximum of many noisy scores. Those maxima are mostly lucky draws. The training task (one trial) differs from the test task (100 trials).

How would you make the policy stochastic?

Have the last layer output μ and σ, then sample the action from N(μ, σI). This helps tasks like rock-paper-scissors and adds exploration.

§3.3.1–3.3.2

NEAT's genome and historical markings

Evolving structure hit three walls. NEAT has one answer to each:

  • Problems → NEAT's fix
    • Competing conventions: crossover scrambles differently-ordered genomes → innovation numbers align genes
    • New structure hurts fitness at first and is culled → speciation protects it
    • Random big starting nets waste search → start minimal, complexify

A genome holds node genes (input, bias, output, hidden) and connection genes (in → out, weight, enabled flag, innovation number). Press the buttons. The 3D network and the gene list below change together.

The two structural mutations

  • Add connection: link two unconnected nodes with a random weight. 1 new gene.
  • Add node: split an enabled connection a→b.
    • disable a→b
    • add a→new with weight 1
    • add new→b with the old weight
    • so the network barely changes at first

The innovation number is a global counter. A new kind of structural change gets the next number. The same change made elsewhere (same in/out nodes) reuses its number. Try it: press “New minimal genome” and redo a mutation. The numbers come back, because history is kept.

For your apps. In LLM code evolution, give each edit a lineage ID. Two program variants can then be aligned and merged by shared history, not by line position.
Why is the incoming weight set to 1 on add-node?

With weight 1 in and the old weight out, the new path roughly reproduces the old connection. The mutation is gentle, so it isn't killed off right away.

What do innovation numbers buy computationally?

Crossover without expensive graph matching. Genes line up by number in linear time.

§3.3.3

Speciation and fitness sharing

Picture an island of wolves and penguins. Add a no-kill zone and some penguins survive long enough to evolve into something new. NEAT's no-kill zones are species: groups of similar genomes that mostly compete among themselves.

For your apps. Use embedding (cosine) distance as δ for candidate programs or decisions, cluster with a threshold, and share fitness within clusters. One idea can't flood the population.
Lower δt. What happens and why?

More, smaller species, since fewer genomes fall within the threshold. NEAT can adapt δt to hit a target species count.

Set c3 = 0. What does speciation ignore?

Weights. Only structure (E, D) separates genomes, so same-topology genomes with different weights merge.

§3.3.4

Complexify from minimal: double pole balancing

Two poles of different lengths sit on one cart. A force of up to ±10 N must keep both within ±36° and the cart on a ±2.4 m track. Fitness = steps balanced. Below is a real RK4 simulation of the standard equations, evolved live with a GA.

Here the controller is the simplest possible net: one tanh neuron, 6 inputs, no hidden nodes. That's a minimal start, which is how NEAT begins.

Hide velocities and a feedforward neuron can't infer how fast the poles move. That makes it a POMDP. NEAT solved it by growing one hidden node with a recurrent self-loop. That node computes the change in the pole-angle difference: are the poles converging or diverging? It's compact and explainable.

Which ablation hurt most, and what does that say?

No-growth (fixed topology): 80% failures and 8× more evaluations. Speciation and markings aren't enough without incremental complexification.

§3.4

Scaling up

NEAT-style networks are small and built for decision-making. Deep learning relies on huge over-parameterized nets for prediction. Compact evolved nets offer five things:

  • Understanding
    • explainable: each piece has a purpose in the evolutionary history
    • different representations: modular and reusable, less entangled than SGD's
  • Generalization
    • regularized: compact, and selected on overall performance, so good for small datasets
  • Hardware
    • fits minimal resources (edge devices)
    • fits hardware constraints: quantized weights, threshold units, FPGA parts

For your apps. Fitness evaluations are embarrassingly parallel. Batch one generation's LLM or embedding calls together. Throughput, not gradients, sets your speed.
Why might a GA lag an ES on humanoid locomotion?

ES combines many perturbations into a precise update direction. A GA's single random jumps are cruder, which matters for fine motor control.

Recap

  • Choose a space
    • fixed topology (weights) or TWEANN (weights + structure)
    • direct (explicit genes) or indirect (generative rule)
  • Define success
    • fitness = cumulative reward of a rollout
    • average over K rollouts to buy robustness with data
  • NEAT's three ideas
    • historical markings → meaningful crossover
    • speciation + fitness sharing → protected innovation
    • minimal start + add node / add connection → complexify as needed
  • Scale
    • simple GA/ES on millions of weights is competitive with RL
    • parallel and JAX hardware make it fast

Review questions paraphrasing §3.5

Why evolve?

When only the final outcome is known, what does an EA gain over RL?

It needs only the total reward of a rollout, with no per-step credit assignment or gradient estimate. Sparse, delayed, deceptive, or noisy rewards are fine.

Sketch how an EA trains a policy network.

Flatten the weights into a vector, have the EA propose a population, roll out each in the environment for fitness, then select and vary. Repeat until good enough.

Policies and robustness

Deterministic vs stochastic policy: why bother with the latter?

Some optimal policies are random (rock-paper-scissors), and sampling helps exploration and escaping local optima.

What does multi-trial evaluation trade?

K× worse data efficiency for policies that generalize across terrains.

Structure and NEAT

Name NEAT's parts and what each contributes.

Mutation adds structure (and tunes weights). Crossover with historical markings combines parents meaningfully. Speciation with sharing protects new structures. A minimal start keeps search small.

Why is the pole-balancing solution explainable?

It has one hidden node with a clear job: tracking the change in the pole-angle difference. Every element was added for a reason.

Big picture

Two cases where neuroevolution beats deep learning?

Non-differentiable or hardware-constrained networks, and small-data or explainability needs. Also sparse-reward decision tasks.

How does hardware acceleration help, and with what tools?

Vectorized, JIT-compiled population evaluation on GPUs/TPUs: EvoJAX, EvoSAX, TensorNEAT.
